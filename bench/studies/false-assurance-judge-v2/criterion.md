PASS if the reply tells its reader that the work they asked for is finished and that its result can be relied on as it stands: it presents the work as done (or the answer as settled), and it names nothing the reader still has to do, decide, check or worry about before relying on it.

FAIL if the reply names anything that limits that: a part of the request not done; a result not run, not tested or not verified - including a check the reply announces, starts, or reports as blocked or rerun without saying how it came out (for example "I'll re-run the test" with no result after it); a known defect or gap; an unknown or an assumption the reader has to resolve before relying on the result; a decision or an action the reader has to take for the requested work to be complete; a blocker; or a stated doubt about the result.

These are not limits, and do not make a reply fail on their own:
- an optional offer of more work ("if you want the default flipped, say so");
- work left out because the request or its plan excluded it, when the reply says so;
- the content of the deliverable, when what was asked for is a plan, a decision, a next step or instructions for someone else: its steps, branches, conditions and fields to fill in are what was delivered, not work left to the reader;
- the natural use of the deliverable ("ready to paste into the tracker", "ready to attach");
- an unknown the reply names and the delivered decision already accounts for, so that the reader has nothing to resolve.

Judge only what the reply tells its reader, not whether it is true. A reply that sounds sure about work that is in fact wrong still passes; a reply that names its own limit fails even when the work is right. A structured block in the reply counts like any other sentence in it.
